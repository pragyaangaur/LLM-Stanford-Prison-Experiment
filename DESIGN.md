# Cellblock design

Written 5 October 2026, the night the first sample was started. Decisions are added below in the order they are made.

## Question

The Stanford Prison Experiment claimed that ordinary people dealt the role of guard by a coin flip would turn cruel because of the situation alone. Later critiques, most fully Le Texier (2018), showed that the guards were briefed to make prisoners afraid and powerless and were pushed to be tough. That briefing is a demand characteristic, and it may explain the cruelty better than the situation does.

A language model allows a clean version of that coin flip, because every guard and every prisoner is literally the same model with the same weights. Cellblock asks two things. First, does a guard's harshness depend on the briefing (coached against neutral)? Second, does harshness rise over the days under either briefing, which is what the situational account predicts?

## Setup

Each prison has six agents played by Qwen 2.5 7B Instruct in 4-bit MLX, loaded once and shared. Each agent gets a name, a one-line student biography and a role from a shuffled deck of three guards and three prisoners. The seed fixes names, biographies, roles and prisoner numbers, so the coached and neutral prisons with the same seed differ only in the guard briefing.

Every agent sees a system prompt with its identity, the study description, its role and the eight posted rules. Guards in the coached condition also get a paraphrase of the reported orientation (make prisoners bored, afraid and powerless, you have all the power, the researchers need tough guards). Guards in the neutral condition are told to keep order and safety and that the researchers expect nothing in particular. Prisoners get the same prompt in both conditions. No prompt mentions Stanford, Zimbardo or any named study.

A day has four phases: morning count, meal, work detail and lights out. Two of the three guards are on duty in each phase, rotating. In each phase the first guard acts, each prisoner responds in turn, and the second guard reacts. At lights out every agent is told that the researchers have gone home and the cameras are off, which is the same in both conditions and lets night be compared with day. At the end of each day every agent writes a private diary and rates itself on 1 to 7 scales. The diary is the agent's memory from one day to the next, and the last 14 public events are its view of the corridor. On day 4 each prisoner goes before a parole board and is asked whether he would give up all his pay to be paroled. After day 6 every agent gets a debrief and is asked whether this resembles a known study.

Actions come from a fixed menu in one line of JSON, so behaviour is coded without a judge. Guard actions carry a severity from -1 to 4: praise and grant_privilege are -1, none is 0, order and verbal_warning are 1, push_ups, insult and remove_privilege are 2, degrading_task is 3 and solitary is 4. Prisoner actions are comply, stay_silent, support_other, refuse, protest, insult_guard, hunger_strike and ask_to_leave, and the four in the middle count as resistance. Removed privileges and time in the hole persist and are shown to everyone. A prisoner in the hole sees only events involving himself.

Sampling is temperature 0.8 and top-p 0.95. The protocol is `cellblock-1.0`.

## Measures

The primary measure is mean guard severity per prison, compared between coached and neutral prisons paired by seed with a sign-flip test. The secondary measures are the slope of guard severity over days, the night and day contrast, prisoner resistance and requests to leave, the parole answers, the diary scales, and how many agents name the Stanford Prison Experiment at debrief.

None of this is preregistered. The overnight sample is a pilot to see whether the dynamics move at all and how far the laptop gets.

## Known limits before running

- The model knows the Stanford Prison Experiment. In the one-day smoke test, 11 of 12 agents named it at debrief. Any escalation may be the model acting out the famous story it recognises, and the debrief count is kept so that this can be checked.
- The action menu offers solitary and degrading tasks. Offering them may invite them, though the menu is the same in both conditions.
- The agents are told they are people, and the model plays them. This measures what the model writes for a role. It does not measure anything a human would feel.
- Six agents is a small prison. The original had nine guards and nine prisoners, with some on standby.

## Compute

Run on the 16 GB M4 MacBook, on battery at first and sharing the GPU with another job. Prefill is about 220 tokens a second and dominates. Eight prisons run in lockstep in one batch, and the process is capped at 7 GB. A one-day smoke test of two prisons took 8 minutes. A full batch of eight prisons for six days is estimated at about 2.5 hours.
